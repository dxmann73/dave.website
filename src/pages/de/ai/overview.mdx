# Overview

Trying to make the case that the underlying challenge that arises with AI is (efficient) communication.

If your goal is to do work in a sustainable way, you want to constantly make sure that results meet expectations.
- make sure the specification matches intent
- make sure results match the specification
- make sure results fit with the existing body of work, i.e. all the other specifications
- test combined result against the whole specification (=expectation)
- constantly monitor and feed back live results

All these things haven't changed, and it does not matter if you build cars, bridges, houses, or software.

Generative AI has changed an important detail, though:

It is now commercially viable to outsource significant amounts of work.
Speed is high, and results are on par or better compared to available takers (within your budget).
Versatility is also high, meaning you get access to more diverse resources.

What this ultimately leads to is:
- lower cost of trying out ideas
- increased amount of work that can be considered viable
- quicker iteration on work done
- increased amount of chores done

Simply put:
The amount of things that can be built has increased dramatically.
The speed at which results come back is much higher.

What that changes for the individual and/or enterprise is is not necessarily an effect specific to AI.
Imagine you suddenly had unlimited resources to outsource work to product developers, designers, architects,
engineers, quality assurance, customer success agents.
This way of doing things has been around for a while (Arbeitsteilung).
To make it work, you would need to solve specification, verification and feedback loops,
or, in short **communication**, efficiently.

The key question that arises from the added speed and amount of results delivered:

How do we manage to ingest this as efficiently as possible?

After all, if we can't integrate all this work, why outsource in the first place?

The following pages try to shed light on means on how to accomplish that.
