# Overview

<Tldr>
- There are certain principles that apply to any product or body of work.
- The use of AI doesn't change these principles, it extends the resources that are available to you.
- This means you can outsource more work than ever before.
- The fundamental challenge when working with AI is therefore communication, verification and trust/confidence.
</Tldr>

## First principles (before AI)

To work in a sustainable way, you want to **constantly** make sure that results meet expectations, i.e. make sure that
- specification matches intent
- results match the specification
- results fit with the existing body of work (= all existing specifications, PM/PO expectations)
- results integrate with the whole existing specification (=user expectation)

It is also a good idea to constantly monitor and feed back live results.

All these things haven't changed, no matter if you happen to build cars, bridges, houses, or software.

## What AI changes

It is now **commercially viable** to outsource significant amounts of work to AI.
- **Speed** of delivery is high.
- Results are on par or better compared to available takers **within your budget**.
- **Versatility** is also high, meaning you get access to **more diverse resources**.

What this ultimately leads to is:
- lower cost of trying out ideas
- increased amount of work that can be considered viable
- quicker iteration on work done
- increased amount of chores done

Simply put:
The amount of things that can be built has increased dramatically.
The speed at which results come back is much higher.

## Unblock your outsourcing

The challenges that arise from that have **nothing** to do with the fact that **AI is doing the work**.

Imagine you suddenly had unlimited resources to outsource work to product developers, designers, architects,
engineers, quality assurance, customer success agents.

This way of doing things has been around for a while (Arbeitsteilung).

To make it work, you would need to solve specification, verification and feedback loops,
or, in short **communication, verification and trust**, efficiently.

So, the key questions that arise from the added speed and amount of results delivered:

- How do we make sure things that get built are what we actually want
- How do we manage to integrate results as efficiently as possible?
- How do we build up trust / confidence that these things actually work?
- How do we feed back desired changes?

After all, if we can't integrate all this work, why outsource in the first place?

The following pages try to shed light on means on how to accomplish that.
